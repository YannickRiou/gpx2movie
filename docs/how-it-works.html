Overview

OpenFlyover imports GPX or FIT tracks, lays them on real terrain covered with aerial photos, and flies a camera over them. You edit a film on a timeline (opening and closing shots, stops, texts, photos, videos, music), then export it as MP4 or WebM, as a still image or as a poster. Everything is computed in the browser: no account, no application server, no API key.

One codebase, two targets

npm run build produces dist/ (≈ 13 MB), a static site served over HTTPS: WebCodecs and crypto.randomUUID require it. The Tauri 2 desktop app (src-tauri/) loads exactly the same dist/. What differs (files, storage, encoder) goes through a single interface, src/platform/.

One core principle: preview = export

The camera, the overlay, the clouds, the water and the weather are pure functions of two numbers: the progress along the track and the film time. The preview calls them for every displayed frame, the export for every frame of the file. What you see is therefore what will be rendered, and the export can compute any frame on its own.

Architecture

The code is organized in layers. At the center, pure modules (without React or three.js where possible) do the computing: the track, the film clock, the camera, the overlay. Around them, zustand stores hold the state. Above, the React interface and the three.js scene read the stores and call the pure modules. At the bottom, the platform layer isolates the browser and Tauri.

Coordinates and reference frames

  • Input in WGS84 lon / lat, in degrees. Tiles follow Web Mercator (XYZ scheme, y = 0 at the north), computed in geo/mercator.ts.
  • Local tangent frame centered on frameOrigin: the center of the first batch of tracks, rounded to 0.01°, fixed as long as one track remains. three.js axes: +X east, +Y up, +Z south.
  • Precision: ECEF → local conversions are done in double precision in JavaScript, never in the shader. Coordinates sent to the GPU stay small, so they are accurate to the millimetre.
  • Heights are above sea level (tiles, GPX, FIT). Only the atmosphere and the clouds, which work in true ECEF, add the EGM96 geoid undulation (+51 m at Chamonix), read from a 1° grid generated by scripts/gen-geoid.mjs.
  • Exaggeration of the terrain: a factor applied to the mesh, the track (+3 m), the water (+2 m), the labels and the clouds.

The path of a frame

preview and export

Every frame, on screen or in the video, follows the same path. The only difference: the preview draws when something moves, while the export draws every frame of the schedule itself and waits until the visible tiles are loaded.

The pipelines, one by one

Each block expands. The steps are listed in the order the program runs them.

Importing a track

src/import/
  1. importFile(file, colorIndex) dispatches to parseGpx or parseFit. Garmin's FIT SDK is loaded only with the first .fit file.
  2. The parsers produce segments of points (lon, lat, ele, time, heart rate, cadence, power, temperature), the waypoints and the local time offset.
  3. buildTrack computes the identifier, the bounding box and the statistics: elevations smoothed over 5 points, 3 m threshold for elevation gain and loss.
  4. A color is assigned (assignColors), chosen to stay readable on aerial photos.
  5. Imagery switches automatically to IGN or swisstopo if the track lies entirely in France or Switzerland.
  6. Consecutive tracks (less than 24 h apart, no overlap) can be chained into one route (chainTracks).

Terrain: tiles and levels of detail

src/terrain/
  1. Catalog (sources.ts): Mapterhorn elevation (default) or AWS Terrarium; imagery from Esri (default), IGN, swisstopo, Sentinel-2, topographic maps, historical IGN photos.
  2. Download (createTileFetcher): 12 requests at a time, duplicates merged, LRU cache of about 600 images, 3 retries (250 ms, 1 s, 4 s). An offline pack is read before the network.
  3. Elevation: each pixel encodes a height. In Terrarium, h = R·256 + G + B/256 − 32768 meters.
  4. Imagery: for an elevation tile at zoom z, the imagery sub-tiles at zoom z+1 are stitched into one texture.
  5. Mesh: a grid of 65 × 65 vertices per tile, each vertex converted lon/lat → height → ECEF → local frame, with a "skirt" along the edges to hide the cracks between levels.
  6. Quadtree (engine.ts): a tile splits when a mesh cell exceeds 3 px on screen. The parent stays displayed until its four children are ready: never a hole. At most 24 concurrent loads, 400 height grids kept (≈ 100 MB).
  7. Loaded area: the tracks' bounding box widened by 25 km, at least 40 km across, covered by at most 16 root tiles.

Flyover, pacing and film clock

src/flyover/ · src/film/clock.ts
  1. buildTrackPath turns the first track into a path that can be sampled (cumulative distances, times).
  2. Pacing (pacing.ts) slows down at highlights: tops of climbs, passes crossed, nearby summits. Speed sections set by hand (×0.25 to ×4) are added on top.
  3. The film clock (buildFilmClock) assembles opening, flight, stops and closing. stateAt(t) tells, at any instant, which phase you are in and where the marker is.
  4. The camera (computeCameraView, then computeFilmView) is a stateless function: five styles (chase, sway, orbit, overhead, cinematic), at least 80 m above the ground, with a clear view of the marker. The opening and closing shots (descent, jump, from the region, sweep) blend the overview into the flight.

Film and timeline

src/film/ · ui/Timeline.tsx
  • The model (settings.film): opening and closing shots, stops (camera "comme le film" (same as the film), slow orbit, wide or fixed view), framings along the track, speed sections, texts, media (photo or video), music tracks, points of interest.
  • Automatic editing (assemble.ts): on load, a 4 s orbit stop at each highlight, and, at passes, summits and huts, a 4 s title and a ×0.5 slow-down over 400 m.
  • The timeline turns gestures (drag, stretch, cut) into pure calls to film/timeline.ts. Each gesture is one undo step.
  • Media: photos placed by their GPS position or their time (EXIF in JPEGs), videos up to 50 MB aligned on their recording time, music tracks with a waveform. The tempo is detected (60–180 BPM) for "Caler sur le rythme" (snap to the beat): titles, stops and slow-down starts land on the beats.

Overlay

src/overlay/

The overlay is not HTML: it is drawn on a 2D canvas by a single pure function, drawOverlay(ctx, frame, settings, size, assets). The preview calls it on a canvas placed over the 3D view; the export calls it on the video frame. The unit is 1% of the shorter side, with a 5% safe margin and 9 positions where elements stack. Three styles (editorial, broadcast, app), with colors and fonts that can be changed on top.

Light, atmosphere, clouds, water, weather

src/scene/ · src/weather/
  • Atmosphere (Takram libraries): physical sky, aerial perspective, then tone mapping, edge smoothing (SMAA) and color grading.
  • Sun at the time recorded under the marker, or at a chosen time; sunrise and sunset from the NOAA equations; terrain shadows in a 4096² shadow map.
  • Volumetric clouds, three layers derived from the real cloud cover (Open-Meteo) or set by hand, pushed by the wind. The preview computes them more coarsely; the export refines them over 2 to 6 passes per frame.
  • Water: OpenStreetMap lakes and rivers within 8 km of the track, with reflections and ripples.
  • Historical weather for the day of the outing, at 2 to 12 points along the track: veiled sun, haze, softer shadows.

Video export

src/export/
  1. The export drawer posts a request to useExportStore.
  2. ExportController pauses playback, stops the render loop (frameloop 'never') and renders at the video size.
  3. For each frame of the schedule, renderSettledFrame waits for the visible tiles (5 s at most), re-drapes the track, renders, and preloads the tiles of the next 40 frames.
  4. The frame is composited on an offscreen canvas: sky gradient, 3D image, overlay.
  5. The encoder takes the first accepted codec: H.264 in MP4, otherwise HEVC, VP9 or VP8 in WebM. Bitrate between 1 and 80 Mbit/s, one keyframe every 2 s.
  6. The file is written to disk while encoding (Chrome, Edge, desktop) in 4 MiB chunks; otherwise it stays in memory.
  7. Sound is mixed separately (OfflineAudioContext, 48 kHz) and encoded as AAC or Opus.
  8. On Linux, the desktop app has no WebCodecs: frames are sent to the system ffmpeg, driven from Rust (src-tauri/src/video.rs).

Variants: PNG or JPEG still image, overlay alone on a transparent background, several formats at once, one film per track in a folder, A4, A3 or square poster at 300 dpi. On the desktop, the one-film-per-track render can also be started from the command line: openflyover --rendu <folder> --sortie <folder> --formats 16:9@1080p (src-tauri/src/cli.rs, src/export/cliRender.ts), with a report and an exit code.

Project, undo and presets

src/project/
  • A project is a single .openflyover.json file: name, settings, film, embedded tracks, media. Version 2, with migrations for older files.
  • On opening, each setting is checked against its default value: a wrong type or an out-of-range value falls back to the default. An old project always opens.
  • The undo history keeps only the changed keys; two changes less than 400 ms apart count as one, and so does a slider drag.
  • "Mes projets" (my projects) saves automatically 3 s after the last change (app folder on the desktop, Cache Storage on the site).

Site or desktop: the platform layer

src/platform/ · src-tauri/

getPlatform() returns the site or the desktop implementation. The rest of the code never touches <input type="file">, localStorage or Tauri directly: it asks the platform to open, save, stream a file to disk, or keep tiles or projects. On the desktop, dialogs are native and the security policy (CSP) lists every allowed server: any new source must be added there.

OpenStreetMap and route planning

src/osm/ · src/route/
  • Overpass: one request per track for landmarks (summits, passes, huts, lakes) and water, only one at a time for the whole app, cached for 30 days.
  • Route planning: the paths of an area are turned into a graph, then an A* search finds the route, preferring trails over roads. Elevations come from the elevation tiles. The result is a track without timestamps.
  • Nominatim turns a place name into coordinates, only when you confirm.

Render on demand

src/scene/renderOnDemand.ts

The 3D view redraws only when something changes. It draws continuously during playback, while tiles are loading and while the view is being reframed; then for 30 frames after each settings change, so the clouds can settle. The time step of one frame is capped at 0.25 s, so playback does not jump after a pause. The export is not affected: it draws every frame itself.

Tests and quality

Unit tests

Vitest, in a simulated DOM (jsdom). Each *.test.ts file sits next to its module: 92 files, 1,466 tests. The network and the encoder are mocked. Command: npx vitest run --maxWorkers=1.

Rust

cargo test in src-tauri/: 9 tests, on the arguments passed to ffmpeg (quality, MP4 or WebM, sound) and on the batch-render command line.

Continuous integration

ci.yml on every push: types, linting, tests, build. desktop.yml on demand: Windows, macOS and Linux installers.

End-to-end tests (npm run e2e)

The e2e/run.mjs script starts its own Vite server, opens the app in a headless Chromium with a software GPU (SwiftShader), and drives it like a user with puppeteer-core. Any console error fails the scenario, except network noise from tiles, weather and OpenStreetMap.

Project rules

  • No API key and no paid service. A new source goes into terrain/sources.ts, docs/sources.md and the CSP in src-tauri/tauri.conf.json.
  • No Node at runtime. The site and the desktop app must both work at all times.
  • Files and storage always go through getPlatform().
  • Strict TypeScript: import type, no enum, no constructor parameter properties. No React in geo/, import/, terrain/.
  • Preview = export: everything that moves depends on the progress and the film time, never on the wall clock.
  • Interface in French; "Carte alpine" (alpine map) style guide (src/ui/theme.css), bundled fonts, Lucide icons.

Known limitations

  • to verify Never seen on a real graphics card: export speed, smoothness, sound, rendering. Development uses software rendering.
  • to verify Desktop app never launched or packaged on a real machine; installer workflows never run.
  • browsers Export not tested on Firefox and Safari; without direct disk writing, the file stays in memory (≈ 2 times its size).
  • licenses Garmin's FIT SDK (not open source) must be settled before a public release; Esri, EOX and Open-Meteo are non-commercial or need review.
  • media Videos of 50 MB at most, embedded in the project; HEIC photos rejected; EXIF read only from JPEGs.
  • rendering Shadows limited to the loaded area; clouds do not cast shadows on the terrain; the weather from a single point applies to the whole scene.
